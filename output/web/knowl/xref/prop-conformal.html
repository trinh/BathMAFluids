Proposition 4.5.2 Properties of conformal maps.

  1. Conformal maps preserve angles.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. If the boundary \(\partial{\hat{R}}\) is a streamline in the \(\zeta\)-plane, then the corresponding boundary \(\partial R\) is a streamline in the \(z\)-plane (and vice versa).
    🔗
    🔗
  3. A source of strength \(Q\) (or vortex of strength \(\Gamma\)) at \(\zeta = g(c) \in \hat{R}\) in the \(\zeta\)-plane corresponds to a source (or vortex) of the same strength at \(z = c \in R\) in the \(z\)-plane (and vice versa).
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof (non-examinable).
(1) Near a point \(z_0\text{,}\) \(g(z) - g(z_0) \approx g'(z_0)(z - z_0)\text{,}\) so small line elements are all rotated by \(\arg g'(z_0)\) and scaled by \(|g'(z_0)|\text{;}\) angles between them are unchanged. (2) If \(\Im F\) is constant on \(\partial\hat{R}\text{,}\) then \(\Im f = \Im F(g(z))\) is constant on \(\partial R\text{.}\) (3) Near \(c\text{,}\) \(g(z) - g(c) = g'(c)(z - c)\big(1 + O(z - c)\big)\text{,}\) so \(\log(g(z) - g(c)) = \log(z - c)\) plus a function analytic near \(c\text{.}\)
