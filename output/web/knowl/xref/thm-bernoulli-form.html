Theorem 3.5.6 The momentum equation in Bernoulli form.

Consider an inviscid, incompressible fluid of constant density \(\rho\text{,}\) subject to a conservative body force \(\bg = -\nabla\chi\text{.}\) Define the Bernoulli function
\begin{equation} B(\bx, t) \equiv \frac{p}{\rho} + \frac{1}{2}|\bu|^2 + \chi.\tag{3.5.4} \end{equation}
Then the momentum equation (3.4.5) can be written as
\begin{equation} \pd{\bu}{t} + \bomega \times \bu = -\nabla B.\tag{3.5.5} \end{equation}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Start from the momentum equation (3.4.5), with \(\bg = -\nabla \chi\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \pd{\bu}{t} + (\bu \cdot \nabla)\bu = - \frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p - \nabla \chi. \end{equation*}
Using the vector identity (3.5.1), and noting that \(\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p = \nabla(p/\rho)\) since \(\rho\) is constant, gives
\begin{equation} \pd{\bu}{t} + (\nabla \times \bu) \times \bu = - \nabla \left(\frac{p}{\rho} + \frac{1}{2} |\bu|^2 + \chi\right).\tag{3.5.6} \end{equation}
Using the definitions of the vorticity (3.5.2) and of \(B\) (3.5.4) gives the result.
