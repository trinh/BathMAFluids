Example 4.2.9 Line vortex.

The radially symmetric streamfunction
\begin{equation} \psi = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\log r,\tag{4.2.8} \end{equation}
defined up to a constant, corresponds to a line vortex. Find its velocity \(\bu\text{,}\) and compute the circulation around a circle of radius \(a\) centred at the origin.
🔗
Solution.
The streamlines are circles of constant \(r\text{,}\) shown in Figure 4.8. From (4.2.6),
\begin{equation} \bu = -\pd{\psi}{r} \be_{\theta} = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r} \be_{\theta},\tag{4.2.9} \end{equation}
so the fluid orbits the origin, with speed \(|\Gamma|/(2\pi r)\) increasing as \(r \to 0\text{.}\)
🔗
The circulation around a contour \(C\) is
\begin{equation*} \oint_C \bu \cdot \de{\bx} = \oint_C (u \, \de{x} + v \, \de{y}), \end{equation*}
i.e. the sum of the velocity components tangential to \(C\text{.}\) By Stokes’ theorem it equals the flux of vorticity through any surface bounded by \(C\text{.}\) Since the flow is irrotational away from the origin, all of this vorticity is concentrated at the origin. On the circle \(r = a\text{,}\) \(\de{\bx} = a \be_{\theta} \de{\theta}\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation} \oint_C \bu \cdot \de{\bx} = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a} \be_{\theta} \cdot \be_{\theta} a \de\theta = \Gamma.\tag{4.2.10} \end{equation}
The quantity \(\Gamma\) is called the vortex strength, analogous to the source strength \(Q\) in Example 4.1.4. Notice that \(\Gamma \gt 0\) corresponds to flow in the anticlockwise sense, and \(\Gamma \lt 0\) to flow in the clockwise sense.
