Corollary 3.2.4.

Let \(V(t)\) be a material volume, let \(\rho\) satisfy the continuity equation (3.2.1), and let \(h(\bx, t)\) be any continuously differentiable function. Then
\begin{equation} \dd{}{t} \iiint_{V(t)} \rho h \, \de{V} = \iiint_{V(t)} \rho \DD{h}{t} \, \de{V}.\tag{3.2.4} \end{equation}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
This proof just follows from expansion. Pass the derivative through the integral and use the formula of Reynolds’ Transport Theorem. Then expand the quantity:
\begin{equation*} \nabla \cdot (f\bu) = \nabla f \cdot \bu + f \nabla \cdot \bu, \end{equation*}
where \(f = \rho h\text{.}\) Finally use the continuity equation on \(\rho\text{.}\)
