Exercise 1. Basic calculations.

The following question relates to two-dimensional potential flow. Remember that the fluid flux through a surface given by contour \(C\) is given by (2.2.2), or
\begin{equation*} \int_C \bu \cdot \bn \, \de{s}. \end{equation*}
You will get some practice on calculating this quantity below.
🔗
For each of the following elementary flows, state or calculate:
  • the complex potential, \(f(z)\text{;}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • the velocity vector, written in vector form \(\bu = [u, v]\text{;}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • the flux through a surface consisting of a circle of unit radius;
    🔗
    Hint: the unit normal for the circle is \(\bn = [\cos\theta, \sin\theta]\text{;}\) when converting to polar coordinates remember that \(\de{s} = r\de{\theta}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • the flux through a surface consisting of a plate given by the line \(y = -x + 1\) with \(0 \leq x \leq 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗

(a)

Uniform flow of velocity \(U\) oriented at an angle of \(\pi/4\) to the horizontal.
🔗
Hint.
Circle. Parametrise the unit circle by \([x, y] = [\cos\theta, \sin\theta]\) for \(0 \leq \theta \lt 2\pi\text{.}\) Explain why the outward unit normal is \(\bn = [\cos\theta, \sin\theta]\) and why \(\de{s} = \de\theta\text{,}\) so that the flux becomes \(\int_0^{2\pi} \bu \cdot \bn \, \de\theta\text{.}\) Before integrating, can you predict the answer?
🔗
Plate. Parametrise the plate by \(\br(t) = [t, 1 - t]\) for \(0 \leq t \leq 1\text{.}\) Show that the tangent is \(\br'(t) = [1, -1]\text{,}\) and hence that a unit normal, pointing away from the origin, is \(\bn = [1, 1]/\sqrt{2}\text{.}\) Show also that \(\de{s} = |\br'(t)| \, \de{t} = \sqrt{2} \, \de{t}\text{,}\) so that the flux becomes \(\int_0^1 \bu \cdot \bn \, \sqrt{2} \, \de{t}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

A line source of strength \(Q\) located at the origin.
🔗
Hint.
For the plate, use the same parametrisation and normal as in part (a), and write the velocity in Cartesian form, \(\bu = \frac{Q}{2\pi}\frac{[x, y]}{x^2 + y^2}\text{.}\) You may use, without proof, that
\begin{equation*} \int_0^1 \frac{\de{t}}{t^2 + (1 - t)^2} = \frac{\pi}{2}. \end{equation*}
