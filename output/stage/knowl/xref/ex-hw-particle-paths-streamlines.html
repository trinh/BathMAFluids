Exercise 5. Particle paths and streamlines.

Aside: Revised in 2026.

Parts (b) and (c) of this question, together with their hints and solutions, were cleaned up for 2026 to set up the pathline and streamline more carefully.

(a)

Define the particle paths and streamlines for a velocity field \(\bu(\bx,t)\text{.}\) When do these coincide?

🔗

(b)

Let \(\bx = \bx(t)\) be a pathline of the fluid, so that \(\dd{\bx}{t} = \bu(\bx(t), t)\) (see Definition 2.2.2), and consider \(f\) evaluated along the pathline,
\begin{equation*} F(t) = f(\bx(t), t), \end{equation*}
which is the value of \(f\) experienced by the fluid particle. By drawing a sketch of the pathline and marking the positions \(\bx\) of the particle at time \(t\) and \(\bx + \de\bx\) at time \(t + \de t\text{,}\) show that
\begin{equation*} \dd{F}{t} = \pd{f}{t} + \bu \cdot \nabla f = \DD{f}{t}. \end{equation*}
Hence show that \(f\) is preserved following the flow if and only if \(\DD{f}{t} = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Since \(\bx(t)\) is a pathline, the displacement of the particle in a small time \(\de t\) is
\begin{equation*} \de\bx = \bu(\bx, t) \, \de t + O(\de t^2). \end{equation*}
By definition of the derivative,
\begin{equation*} \dd{F}{t} = \lim_{\de t \to 0} \frac{f(\bx + \de\bx, t + \de t) - f(\bx, t)}{\de t}. \end{equation*}
To expand the numerator, use the Taylor series for a function of several variables:
\begin{equation*} f(\bx + \de\bx, t + \de t) = f(\bx, t) + \de\bx \cdot \nabla f(\bx, t) + \de t \, \pd{f}{t}(\bx, t) + O\big(|\de\bx|^2, \, |\de\bx| \, \de t, \, \de t^2\big), \end{equation*}
where, in components, \(\de\bx \cdot \nabla f = \de x \, \pd{f}{x} + \de y \, \pd{f}{y} + \de z \, \pd{f}{z}\text{.}\) Substitute for \(\de\bx\) and let \(\de t \to 0\text{.}\)

(c)

Let \(\bx = \bx(s, t)\) be a streamline of the flow at fixed time \(t\text{,}\) so that \(\pd{\bx}{s} = \bu(\bx(s, t), t)\text{,}\) where \(s\) is the parameter along the streamline (see Definition 2.2.1). Consider \(f\) evaluated on the streamline,
\begin{equation*} G(s) = f(\bx(s, t), t). \end{equation*}
Show that \(f\) is constant along the streamline if and only if
\begin{equation*} \bu \cdot \nabla f = 0. \end{equation*}
🔗
Hint.
Consider the points \(\bx = \bx(s, t)\) and \(\bx + \de\bx = \bx(s + \de s, t)\) on the same streamline, at the same instant \(t\text{.}\) Since the curve is a streamline,
\begin{equation*} \de\bx = \bu(\bx, t) \, \de s + O(\de s^2). \end{equation*}
By definition of the derivative,
\begin{equation*} \dd{G}{s} = \lim_{\de s \to 0} \frac{f(\bx + \de\bx, t) - f(\bx, t)}{\de s}. \end{equation*}
Because the time is the same at both points, the Taylor series now has no time term:
\begin{equation*} f(\bx + \de\bx, t) = f(\bx, t) + \de\bx \cdot \nabla f(\bx, t) + O(|\de\bx|^2). \end{equation*}
