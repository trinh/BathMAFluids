Example 4.3.8 Line source.

Show that the complex potential of the line source of Example 4.1.4 is
\begin{equation} f(z) = \frac{Q}{2\pi} \log z,\tag{4.3.5} \end{equation}
and recover \(\phi\text{,}\) \(\psi\) and \(\bu\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
The complex logarithm is not analytic at \(z = 0\text{,}\) a branch point, but is analytic away from the origin once a branch cut is chosen. Writing \(z = r\e^{\im \theta}\) in polar form, it is evaluated via
\begin{equation} \log z = \log r + \im \theta.\tag{4.3.6} \end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation*} \phi = \Re[f] = \frac{Q}{2\pi} \log r \quad \text{and} \quad \psi = \Im[f] = \frac{Q}{2\pi} \theta. \end{equation*}
Indeed, the streamlines are the rays \(\theta = \textrm{constant}\text{.}\) From (4.3.4), multiplying the top and bottom by the conjugate of \(z\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} u - \im v = \frac{Q}{2\pi} \frac{1}{z} = \frac{Q}{2\pi} \frac{x - \im y}{x^2 + y^2}. \end{equation*}
