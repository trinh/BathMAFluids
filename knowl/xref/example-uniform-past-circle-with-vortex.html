Example 4.6.3 Uniform flow past a cylinder with circulation.

Consider the flow of Example 4.6.2, with complex potential
\begin{equation} w(z) = Uz + \frac{Ua^2}{z}.\tag{4.6.3} \end{equation}
🔗

(a)

Confirm, by use of the streamfunction \(\psi\text{,}\) that the circle boundary is a streamline of the flow.
🔗
It will be useful to remind yourself that
\begin{equation} \cos\theta = \frac{\e^{\im\theta} + \e^{-\im\theta}}{2}. \tag{4.6.4} \end{equation}
🔗
Solution.
We set \(z = a\e^{\im\theta}\) into the complex potential. This gives
\begin{equation*} \phi + \im \psi = U a \left( \e^{\im \theta} + \e^{-\im\theta}\right) = 2Ua\cos\theta. \end{equation*}
Therefore \(\psi = \Im w = 0\) on the surface of the cylinder.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

Derive the complex velocity, \(u - \im v\text{,}\) and use it to show that on the surface of the cylinder, the velocity is given by
\begin{equation*} u - \im v = 2U \e^{\im (\pi/2 - \theta)} \sin\theta, \end{equation*}
where \(z = a\e^{\im\theta}\text{.}\)
🔗
It is helpful for you to remember that
\begin{equation} \sin\theta = \frac{\e^{\im\theta} - \e^{-\im\theta}}{2\im}.\tag{4.6.5} \end{equation}
🔗
Solution.
Differentiating gives the complex velocity,
\begin{equation*} w'(z) = u - \im v = U\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{z^2}\right). \end{equation*}
Substituting \(z = a\e^{\im\theta}\) and simplifying gives
\begin{equation*} u - \im v = U \e^{-\im \theta}\left( \e^{\im\theta} - \e^{-\im\theta}\right) = 2U \e^{\im(\pi/2 - \theta)} \sin\theta. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗
🔗

(c)

Conclude that on the surface of the cylinder, the maximum velocity is \(|\bu| = 2U\text{.}\) Where does this maximum velocity occur? Where do stagnation points in the flow (where the velocity is zero) occur?
🔗
Solution.
From the above, the magnitude is \(|u - \im v| = 2U|\sin\theta|\text{,}\) so the maximum speed on the cylinder is \(2U\text{.}\) This value is obtained at the top and bottom, \(\theta = \pm \pi/2\text{.}\) The stagnation points are where the speed is zero, at the fore and aft points, \(\theta = \pi\) and \(\theta = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(d)

Show that on the surface of the cylinder, the pressure is given by
\begin{equation*} p = p_\infty + \frac{1}{2} \rho U^2(1 - 4\sin^2\theta), \end{equation*}
where \(p_\infty\) is the pressure at infinity.
🔗
Solution.
From Bernoulli’s equation (Theorem 3.5.8), without gravity,
\begin{equation*} \left(p + \frac{\rho}{2} |\bu|^2\right)_{\text{cylinder}} = \left(p_\infty + \frac{\rho}{2} U^2\right)_{\text{infinity}}, \end{equation*}
where \(p_\infty\) is the pressure at infinity. Substituting the speed on the cylinder, \(|\bu|^2 = 4U^2\sin^2\theta\text{,}\) gives
\begin{equation*} p = p_\infty + \frac{\rho}{2}U^2(1 - 4\sin^2\theta) \end{equation*}
as desired.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(e)

It is possible to add a line vortex to the interior of the circular cylinder, centred at \(z = 0\text{,}\) by adding to (4.6.3) an additional term:
\begin{equation*} w(z) = U \left(z + \frac{a^2}{z}\right) - \frac{\im\Gamma}{2\pi} \log z. \end{equation*}
Verify again that the above modification does not change the streamline properties of \(|z| = a\text{.}\)
🔗
Does the above flow satisfy the condition that the flow is of uniform speed \(U\) far upstream?
🔗
Solution.
The streamfunction of the vortex is constant on circles centred at the origin, so the cylinder remains a streamline. Indeed, on the surface, the added term is
\begin{equation*} -\frac{\im \Gamma}{2\pi} (\log a + \im \theta) = \frac{\Gamma\theta}{2\pi} - \frac{\im\Gamma}{2\pi}\log a. \end{equation*}
Therefore the imaginary part is still a constant, and hence the boundary of the cylinder is still a streamline of the flow.
🔗
To verify that the upstream flow is not altered, we take a derivative to obtain the complex velocity:
\begin{equation*} w'(z) = u - \im v = U\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{z^2}\right) - \frac{\im\Gamma}{2\pi z}. \end{equation*}
So in the limit \(|z| \to \infty\text{,}\) the velocity is horizontal with speed \(U\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(f)

By studying the velocity of the flow with the vortex added, verify that the complex velocity on the surface is given by
\begin{equation*} u - \im v = \im \e^{-\im\theta}\left(2U \sin\theta - \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a}\right). \end{equation*}
🔗
Conclude that a point on the cylinder that satisfies the equation
\begin{equation*} \sin\theta = \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi a U}, \end{equation*}
is a stagnation point of the flow.
🔗
Solution.
Using the previous task and adding the vortex term,
\begin{equation*} u - \im v = 2U \im \e^{-\im\theta}\sin\theta - \frac{\Gamma\im}{2\pi a} \e^{-\im\theta} = \im \e^{-\im\theta} \left(2U \sin\theta - \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a}\right). \end{equation*}
🔗
A stagnation point requires that \(|u - \im v| = 0\text{,}\) so setting the terms in brackets to zero implies
\begin{equation*} \sin\theta = \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi a U}, \end{equation*}
as desired. This requires \(|\Gamma| \le 4\pi aU\text{;}\) for larger \(|\Gamma|\text{,}\) the stagnation point leaves the cylinder.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(g)

(Not examinable) Use a computational tool to investigate the streamlines of the flow for \(\Gamma = 0\text{,}\) \(2\pi aU\text{,}\) \(4\pi a U\text{,}\) and \(6\pi a U\text{.}\)
🔗
The fact that the stagnation point shifts, moving on the top or bottom of the circular cylinder, depending on the value of \(\Gamma\text{,}\) will result in an asymmetry in the pressure, causing a net force on one side of the cylinder. This is related to the well-known Magnus effect, which is used by athletes to make a ball’s path curve.
🔗
Solution.
The point of this task is to note that when \(\Gamma \neq 0\text{,}\) the location of the stagnation point shifts. For example, if \(\Gamma = 4\pi a U\text{,}\) then the stagnation points are found at
\begin{equation*} \sin\theta = 1 \Longrightarrow \theta = \pi/2, \end{equation*}
i.e. the top of the cylinder. For \(\Gamma = 6\pi aU\text{,}\) the stagnation point lies off the cylinder.
