Theorem 3.3.2 Forces in inviscid fluids.

In the case of inviscid fluids, we assume that the surface force exerted on (interior) volume elements is accounted for solely by a pressure, \(p\text{,}\) which acts in the inward normal direction at each point, with
\begin{equation*} F_n \bn = -p \bn. \end{equation*}
🔗
Consequently, the surface force, given by (3.3.4), is
\begin{equation} \iint_{\partial V} (-p) \bn \, \de{S} = \iiint_V (-\nabla p) \, \de{V}.\tag{3.3.5} \end{equation}
🔗
In particular, for the case of inviscid fluids, we ignore tangential internal forces.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Apply the divergence theorem to each component, using \(\nabla \cdot (-p\be_i) = -\pd{p}{x_i}\) for \(i = 1, 2, 3\text{.}\)
