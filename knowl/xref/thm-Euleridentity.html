Theorem 3.1.2 Euler’s identity.

The material derivative of the Jacobian of the transformation \(\bx = \bx(\bX, t)\) is given by
\begin{equation} \DD{J}{t} = J \nabla \cdot \bu,\tag{3.1.1} \end{equation}
where \(\bu = \DD{\bx}{t}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
(Not examinable) The material derivative is the time derivative with \(\bX\) held fixed, so \(\pd{x}{t} = u\text{,}\) \(\pd{y}{t} = v\) and \(\pd{z}{t} = w\text{.}\) By the product rule, \(\DD{J}{t}\) is a sum of three determinants, each with one row of \(J\) differentiated.
🔗
Consider the first. A typical entry of its differentiated row is
\begin{equation*} \DD{}{t}\pd{x}{X} = \pd{u}{X} = \pd{u}{x}\pd{x}{X} + \pd{u}{y}\pd{y}{X} + \pd{u}{z}\pd{z}{X}, \end{equation*}
using the chain rule; the entries for \(Y\) and \(Z\) are the same with \(X\) replaced. So the new first row is
\begin{equation*} \pd{u}{x}\,(\text{row 1}) + \pd{u}{y}\,(\text{row 2}) + \pd{u}{z}\,(\text{row 3}). \end{equation*}
A determinant with two equal rows vanishes, so only the first term survives, and this determinant equals \(\pd{u}{x}J\text{.}\)
🔗
In the same way, the other two determinants give \(\pd{v}{y}J\) and \(\pd{w}{z}J\text{.}\) Adding them gives \(\DD{J}{t} = J\,\nabla\cdot\bu\text{.}\)
