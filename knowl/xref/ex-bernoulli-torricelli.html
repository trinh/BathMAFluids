Example 3.5.13 Torricelli’s law.

A large open tank of cross-sectional area \(A\) holds water of depth \(h(t)\) above a small hole of area \(a \ll A\) in its base.
🔗
  1. Find the speed \(u\) of the jet leaving the hole.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. If the initial depth is \(h_0\text{,}\) find the time for the tank to drain.
    🔗
    🔗
Solution.
(a) The free surface falls at speed \(U\text{,}\) where mass conservation gives \(AU = au\text{,}\) so \(U/u = a/A \ll 1\text{.}\) The flow therefore changes slowly, and we treat it as steady. Taking a streamline from the free surface (\(z = h\)) to the jet (\(z = 0\)), where the pressure is atmospheric at both ends, Theorem 3.5.7 gives
\begin{equation*} \frac{p_\text{atm}}{\rho} + \frac{1}{2}U^2 + gh = \frac{p_\text{atm}}{\rho} + \frac{1}{2}u^2. \end{equation*}
Using \(U = (a/A)u\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} u = \sqrt{\frac{2gh}{1 - (a/A)^2}} \approx \sqrt{2gh}. \end{equation*}
This is Torricelli’s law (1643): the jet leaves at the speed of a particle falling freely from height \(h\text{.}\)
🔗
(b) Mass conservation gives
\begin{equation*} A \dd{h}{t} = -a\sqrt{2gh}. \end{equation*}
Separating variables and using \(h(0) = h_0\) gives
\begin{equation*} \sqrt{h} = \sqrt{h_0} - \frac{a}{A}\sqrt{\frac{g}{2}} \, t, \end{equation*}
so the tank empties at time
\begin{equation*} T = \frac{A}{a}\sqrt{\frac{2h_0}{g}}. \end{equation*}
🔗
In practice, the jet from a sharp-edged hole contracts just after leaving it (the vena contracta), and the measured flow rate is about \(0.6\) of this prediction.
